# Research Ethics and Claims Boundary

## Positioning

This is a software-and-methods project that studies **model behavior under controlled synthetic inputs**. It is not research on autistic children and not a substitute for clinical or participatory autism research.

## Ethical principles

- Respect: use non-stigmatizing language and make synthetic status visible.
- Beneficence: prevent distressing content from being framed as a recommendation.
- Justice: avoid presenting five fictional profiles as representative of the full autistic population.
- Transparency: record provenance and disclose model uncertainty.
- Participation: future real-world extensions require appropriate expert, autistic-community, guardian, institutional, and ethics review.

## Language

Use “autistic person” or the individual's preferred terminology. Avoid deficit-only descriptions. Do not use a single high/medium/low functioning label as the primary representation of support needs.

## Human-subjects boundary

The packaged project uses fictional personas and synthetic outputs only. If the project later collects interviews, observations, identifiable records, surveys, recordings, or intervention data from real people, stop and obtain institutional guidance before proceeding.

## Public-claim template

> We built an educational simulator that produces structured synthetic hypotheses from fictional persona profiles and music parameters. The results demonstrate software behavior and prompt sensitivity, not clinical effectiveness or individual prediction.
